# Film storyboard — Ve-AP in the car

**Film:** *Ve-AP: a governed AI agent in a real car*. Subtitles only; each clip keeps its own sound, which
includes the AI's spoken answers. The film opens with the project slides, then plays the vehicle footage.
**Recorded:** 2026-10-19, between 15:06 and 15:51, a Hyundai hatchback on local roads. The test rig was on
the passenger side: a Jetson Orin Nano, a Raspberry Pi 5 with its 7-inch display, a dash cam, a driver
webcam, and the car's own audio over Bluetooth.

## Source footage

| Clip | Length | What it shows | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — phone, bring-up and drowsiness | 2:21 | One command starts both computers; a tour of the rig; the head unit paired over Bluetooth; driver monitoring parked, then a drowsiness alert | the user's narration |
| B — phone, road view | 1:27 | The cabin display's Camera view while driving: dash cam picture, detections, lane model | narration |
| C — phone, ask the car + attention while driving | 4:44 | Voice questions and spoken answers while moving; a tour of the cabin pages; driver attention while driving; the road view close up | the question, then the AI's spoken answer |
| D — screen recording of the cabin display | 12:45 | The same drive as clip C, captured from the display itself (sharp); no sound | none |

Clips C and D overlap. For C's first 3:15, C runs 43 s behind D. That offset was matched on events both clips
show: the page switches and the drowsiness banner. In that span they are shown together: D large, and C as a
picture-in-picture that carries the sound. Each spoken answer starts 0.5–3 s after its text appears, because
speech is synthesised and the car audio adds a lead-in. After 3:15, C has a cut, so it is not synced.

## Subtitles

- Each chapter opens with one line of context: what is being shown and why it matters.
- The user's narration is subtitled from the whisper.cpp transcript, corrected by hand, for example
  *VEP* → *Ve-AP*, *drowsiness*, *Jetson Orin Nano*, *GNSS*, *Wi-Fi*, *openpilot*.
- The AI's spoken answers are subtitled word for word, so a viewer can check them against the screen.
- The same text goes into `captions.srt` for YouTube CC and is burned into the film.

## What the footage shows honestly

- **Real in the car:** the dash cam, the driver webcam and DMS, voice in and spoken answers out over the
  car's audio, the cabin display, and both computers on their own battery.
- **Simulated:** the vehicle signals on screen (speed, battery %, EV range, coolant). They come from the CAN
  simulator on the Pi, and the screen marks them *estimate* / *replayed*. Reading the car's own OBD-II port is
  the next stage, and it was not part of this drive.
- **Not working on the day:**
  - GNSS had no fix; the cause is not established.
  - The bench panel was not connected.
  - The road camera lens was never calibrated, so the lane model ran on an assumed lens.
